Who does what, and where money leaks

You run a club that sends helpers to clean 2,000 buildings. Owners pay you, and you pay helpers. Some buildings cost more than they pay. One pays an old, low price. One is so far away the helper drives almost an hour. At one, the door is always locked and the helper gets blamed. So we make a report card for every building with a sticker saying why it loses. We practice on a pretend town with hidden problems, then try fixes in some neighborhoods and compare with the rest.

The players

Customer HQ / National Account Buyer

A parent who hires one cleaning company for all the family's houses and wants one simple bill.

Role The corporate client (retail chain, bank, clinic network) whose procurement team signs one master agreement for many sites and pushes for one blended rate. It sets the SLA and credit rules and pays net 45-60 (illustrative).

Gives Site list, master agreement and scope, SLA and credit rules, monthly fees, and price pressure at every rebid.

Gets Clean sites, one contact and one invoice, photo and inspection proof, and the power to rebid and keep only the sites that are cheap to serve.

Customer Site Contact (store, branch or clinic manager)

The person living in the house who holds the key, notices a dirty bathroom, and asks the helpers to 'also wipe the fridge.'

Role The person at the location who controls keys, alarm codes and time windows and raises tickets and extra requests. Wrong or missing site details cause late or missed visits that get blamed on the crew (ACCESS-INFO), and informal asks cause SCOPE-CREEP.

Gives Access details, time windows, tickets, informal extra requests and renewal feedback. Under Bhavani's idea of a site passport, also a verified map pin, entrance photo, building/block/floor/suite, access instructions and a contact before service starts.

Gets A site cleaned and photo-verified before opening, heads-up messages, quick fixes, and often free extra work without knowing it is outside the contract.

Commercial Team: Sales, Estimating and Account Management

The person who promises the family a price before seeing the houses, plus the friendly go-between who says sorry when something goes wrong.

Role Sales prices bids, often for hundreds of sites at once with guessed sq ft and no site walk (assumption, verify in Phase 1), and those prices last for years. Account managers handle escalations, approve credits, quote extras and negotiate renewals.

Gives Bid prices, scope sheets, assumed production rates, escalator terms, credit approvals, extra-work quotes and renewal deals.

Gets Sales is paid on revenue, so below-cost sites still earn commission (UNDERPRICED). Account managers are scored on satisfaction, so they give credits and free extras without recovering the cost (SCOPE-CREEP, NO-OWNER).

Operations, Dispatch and Vendor Procurement

The planner who picks which helper team cleans which house, and finds a new team fast when one doesn't show up.

Role Operations assigns vendors, builds schedules, handles misses and finds backup cover. Procurement recruits and vets vendors, checks insurance and compliance, and negotiates rate cards with minimum-visit and trip charges.

Gives Schedules, vendor awards, rate cards, emergency dispatches and corrective-action plans. The recommended model is a zone anchor vendor plus a named backup vendor for Core and Fringe sites.

Gets Peazy completion and issue alerts, and scorecards on coverage (operations) and fill rate (procurement). One costly vendor per remote site therefore counts as success (VENDOR-FRAGMENTED, ISOLATED, NO-OWNER).

QA Inspectors

The checker who walks in and gives the house a cleanliness report card.

Role Company field auditors, customer inspectors or vendor self-inspection score cleanliness by area. They log deficiencies and trigger re-cleans and corrective actions.

Gives Inspection scores, deficiency lists, photos and re-clean requests.

Gets Salary plus mileage (assumption, verify in Phase 1), so visits drift to easy, nearby sites and remote loss sites are inspected least (NO-OWNER). Their field time is a real cost that belongs in M2.

Finance (Billing/AR, Payables/AP, FP&A)

The money keeper who sends the family the bill and pays the helper companies.

Role Invoices customers, pays vendors, books credits and chargebacks, and reports margin by customer or region rather than by site. Its ERP data is not joined to Peazy and has no shared location ID (assumption, verify in Phase 1; DATA-QUALITY).

Gives Customer invoices, vendor payments, credit memos, margin reports and payment terms (collect net 45-60, pay net 15-30, illustrative).

Gets Contract prices from sales and vendor invoices often lumped across sites, but no visit proof, so it can't easily ask 'did we pay for a visit that never happened?' (UNVERIFIED-VISITS).

Vendor Company (subcontracted local janitorial business)

The local helper company that sends the people who clean, and the cleaning company pays it, not the family.

Role Usually a small owner-run business that employs the cleaners, sets wages, owns the equipment and dispatches its own crews. On the reference site it earns about $2,100/month at a ~$29/hr bill rate (illustrative) and, on fixed-fee contracts, profits by finishing in fewer hours than priced.

Gives Labor, supervision, equipment, chemicals, travel, insurance, and Peazy check-ins and photos. Its own dispatcher owns the routes; ours are only optimizer suggestions.

Gets Monthly or per-visit pay, periodic and extra-work pay, and minimum-visit or trip charges. If underpaid, it cuts time on site, sends smaller crews, re-subcontracts or drops the site (LOW-PRODUCTIVITY, UNVERIFIED-VISITS).

Vendor Crew Lead and Cleaners (night crew)

The helpers with the mops who drive from house to house at night.

Role Night crews clean to a checklist, check in through the Peazy app, take photos and drive between sites. Turnover is high, and new crews are slower and miss tasks (CREW-CHURN).

Gives About 3.3 labor hours per visit on the reference site at ~3,000 sq ft/hr, check-ins, photos, and notes on problems like a blocked entrance (illustrative).

Gets About $17/hr, often with unpaid drive time and blame for misses caused by bad site info. Bhavani's two-way on-time promise uses geofence reason codes to protect them from unfair blame.

Specialty, Emergency and Supplies Partners

Extra helpers for big jobs or emergencies, and the store that sells the soap and paper towels.

Role Floor-care, carpet and window specialists, backup crews that step in when the main vendor misses, and suppliers of paper, liners, soap and chemicals. They fit the recommended marketplace layer for periodic, emergency and Isolated work.

Gives Periodic and project work, backup and same-night cover, consumables and equipment.

Gets Project fees, a backup step-in premium (~+15-30%) or same-night emergency rate (~1.5-2x), and supply payments (illustrative). The customer price rarely covers these (assumption, verify in Phase 1; PERIODIC-GAP, PREMIUM-COVER, SUPPLIES-LEAK).

Life of one location

  1. 1. Bid and price the location (usually inside a multi-site deal)

    Someone promises the family a price for cleaning each house, often before looking inside.

    Data captured Estimated sq ft, building type, frequency, scope sheet, price, assumed production rate and hours, term and escalators. Mostly in CRM or spreadsheets, not Peazy.

    Money leaks Guessed sq ft, blended rates that ignore travel and local wages, and missing escalators leave prices below should-cost, like the losing twin's legacy $2,300 (illustrative; UNDERPRICED). Sales is paid on revenue, so nobody is rewarded for site margin (NO-OWNER).

  2. 2. Contract and scope (SOW and SLAs)

    Write down exactly which rooms get cleaned, how often, and what happens if someone messes up.

    Data captured Task list by area, frequency, periodic schedule, SLA and credit rules, extras pricing, and who pays for consumables. Under Bhavani's two-way promise, also a clause that customer-caused misses stay billable.

    Money leaks Vague scope ('as needed') invites SCOPE-CREEP, and periodic work or consumables get bundled in 'free' (PERIODIC-GAP, SUPPLIES-LEAK). Customer credit rules are stricter than vendor chargebacks, so the company eats the difference.

  3. 3. Vendor sourcing and award

    Find a local helper company near the house and agree what to pay it.

    Data captured Density tier, zone anchor vendor and named backup, rate card, pay basis (monthly or per visit), minimum-visit and trip charges, and insurance and compliance documents.

    Money leaks One vendor per scattered site means minimums of $60-120 per visit or a 1-2 hour floor with no route density (illustrative; VENDOR-FRAGMENTED, ISOLATED). Procurement is scored on fill rate, so any vendor that covers the site looks fine (NO-OWNER).

  4. 4. Site onboarding (site passport)

    Put the house on the map, draw the invisible fence, and write down exactly how to get in.

    Data captured Bhavani's site passport, complete before the first visit: verified map pin, entrance photo, building/block/floor/suite, access instructions and contact. Plus geofence, schedule, checklist, and verified sq ft and fixtures.

    Money leaks Wrong pins and missing keys or alarm codes cause wasted trips and late or missed visits that get blamed on the crew (ACCESS-INFO, CREW-CHURN). Unverified sq ft hides pricing errors (DATA-QUALITY).

  5. 5. Scheduling and route suggestions

    We pick the helper company for each building and suggest an order, and the helper company decides its own route.

    Data captured Nightly schedule, time windows, assigned vendor (the vendor assigns its own crew; Peazy records the crew lead who checks in and the roster headcount), density tier and suggested route order.

    Money leaks Rigid windows and one-site trips push travel share above 33% (illustrative; ISOLATED), and two vendors may serve neighboring sites (VENDOR-FRAGMENTED). Optimized routes stay suggestions that the vendor's own dispatcher owns, to limit joint-employer risk.

  6. 6. Nightly service

    The helpers arrive, clean, take pictures, and leave.

    Data captured Geofence entry and exit, dwell time, checklist completion, photos, crew notes, and a reason code for any late or missed visit.

    Money leaks Hours run over standard (LOW-PRODUCTIVITY), or short and ghost visits still get paid (UNVERIFIED-VISITS). Unpaid drive time and unfair blame push crews to quit, and new crews are slower and miss tasks (CREW-CHURN).

  7. 7. Periodic work, extras and service issues

    The family asks for extra jobs or complains, and every few months the big jobs like shining floors get done.

    Data captured Periodic schedule and completion, tickets by category and priority, response time, emergency dispatches and change orders.

    Money leaks Floor care is paid to vendors but never billed (PERIODIC-GAP), and extras are done free to please the site contact (SCOPE-CREEP). Same-night emergency call-outs cost about 1.5-2x the normal rate (illustrative; PREMIUM-COVER).

  8. 8. Inspection, re-cleans and credits

    The checker grades the house, and if it fails, the helpers come back and the family may get money off.

    Data captured Area scores, deficiencies, photos, re-clean tickets, credits issued, corrective actions and the miss reason code.

    Money leaks Re-cleans and credits cost the losing twin about $150/month and are rarely recovered from the vendor (illustrative; RECLEAN-CREDITS). Inspectors favor easy sites, so loss sites stay unseen (NO-OWNER).

  9. 9. Billing and payment (vendor AP and customer AR)

    We pay the helper company and send the family its bill, but the family pays us later than we pay the helpers.

    Data captured Vendor invoices (often lumped across sites), visits claimed, trip and minimum charges, chargebacks, customer invoices, extras billed, credits, DSO and DPO.

    Money leaks Visits are paid without a three-way match of schedule, verified visit and invoice (UNVERIFIED-VISITS), and lumped invoices hide each site's cost (DATA-QUALITY). Customers pay net 45-60 while vendors get net 15-30 (illustrative), so the company floats about a month of vendor cost, an M3 carry.

  10. 10. Review, renew, reprice or exit

    Once a year, decide if each house needs a new price, a different cleaning plan, or a goodbye.

    Data captured Per-site M1, M2 and M3 trend, should-cost gap, root-cause codes, quality and ticket trends, and wage inflation since signing.

    Money leaks Contracts renew at the old price despite wage increases (UNDERPRICED), and with no owner of site margin nobody pushes a reprice or rescope (NO-OWNER). Any exit or rescope must raise account-level M2 dollars, not just M2 %, so shrinking can't fake success.

Where they are facing problems

The 11 problem pins

These are the exact spots where the business loses money. Each pin has a number that matches the red dots on the diagrams.

1

Contracts priced too low

At the sale: the fee the customer pays

UNDERPRICED PERIODIC-GAP

Some buildings were sold for less than it costs to clean them, so they lose money from day one.

What goes wrong Sales quotes site by site with a flat price per sq ft, often bidding hundreds of sites at once with guessed square footage. Travel, vendor minimums and periodic floor care are left out, and legacy contracts have no escalator while wages rise.

How you would spot it in data Fee below should-cost; margin negative since the contract started; no escalator clause; periodic work billed by the vendor but not to the customer.

Fix A should-cost pricing engine, a deal-desk margin gate before signing, and re-pricing at renewal.

2

Scope creep

At the customer site

SCOPE-CREEP

The customer keeps asking for extra jobs, the crew does them, and nobody pays for them.

What goes wrong Site managers add tasks informally (break-room fridge, patio sweep, glass doors). The vendor bills extras or takes longer while the customer's fee stays the same.

How you would spot it in data Service requests that mention out-of-scope tasks; vendor extras with no matching customer invoice line; time on site creeping up month after month.

Fix A digital scope list per site, plus AI that reads service requests and flags out-of-scope work so it gets quoted, billed or declined.

3

Cleaning more often than needed

In the cleaning schedule

OVER-SERVICED

Some buildings get cleaned every night even when hardly anyone is there.

What goes wrong Visit frequency was set at signing (say, five nights a week) and never matched to real use. With hybrid work, many offices are half empty.

How you would spot it in data Short visits together with high inspection scores, few tickets and low occupancy. (Short visits with falling scores mean the opposite problem: pin 6.)

Fix Match frequency to occupancy and offer service tiers, keeping the price per visit healthy.

4

Many small vendors, priced per site

Between the company and its vendors

VENDOR-FRAGMENTED

Lots of small helper companies each charge a minimum fee, so small or far-away buildings cost too much.

What goes wrong Sites are spread across many vendors. Each one quotes per site with trip minimums ($60-120 or a 1-2 hour floor, illustrative), so the company gets no density discount and pays each vendor's overhead separately.

How you would spot it in data Minimum-charge lines on vendor bills; high vendor cost per sq ft where a vendor has few nearby sites; several vendors serving the same zone.

Fix Group sites into zones with one anchor vendor and a named backup vendor on zone pricing.

Covered in Q3 Q4

5

Too much driving

On the road between buildings

ISOLATED

Crews spend paid time driving between buildings that are far apart.

What goes wrong Where sites are sparse, travel time and mileage get built into vendor rates and nobody plans routes across sites. Isolated means more than 45 night drive-minutes to the nearest other site (illustrative).

How you would spot it in data Travel share = drive minutes / (drive + on-site minutes). Above 33% is a red flag (illustrative).

Fix Zone clustering and suggested routes that the vendor's own dispatcher owns; swap, re-price or hand off isolated sites.

6

Slow, short or skipped visits

Inside the building

LOW-PRODUCTIVITY UNVERIFIED-VISITS

Some crews take much longer than others, and some visits are paid for but were rushed or never happened.

What goes wrong There is no productivity standard (routine offices run about 2,500-4,000 sq ft per hour, illustrative), and payment is not tied to verified visits, so slow work and short visits both get paid in full.

How you would spot it in data Dwell minutes x crew size below 60% of standard labor minutes on 3+ visits in 30 days, with falling scores or missing photos (illustrative rule).

Fix Productivity standards by space type, and vendor pay only for geofence- and photo-verified visits.

7

Re-cleans, credits and emergency cover

After the cleaning, or when a vendor doesn't show

RECLEAN-CREDITS PREMIUM-COVER

When a job is done badly, someone pays to clean it again, and when helpers don't come, the replacement costs extra.

What goes wrong Failed inspections lead to re-cleans, credits and lost customers, and contracts rarely make the vendor pay for poor work. A vendor no-show means a backup crew at +15-30% or a same-night emergency call-out at 1.5-2x (illustrative).

How you would spot it in data Inspection failure rate, repeat tickets, credits issued with no vendor chargeback, premium spend as a share of vendor cost.

Fix AI photo checks, vendor scorecards, and contracts with bonuses for good scores and chargebacks for re-cleans.

8

Data that doesn't connect

Between Peazy and the finance systems

DATA-QUALITY SUPPLIES-LEAK

The app knows the cleaning happened but not what it cost, so nobody can see which buildings lose money.

What goes wrong Peazy tracks completion, check-ins, photos and issues, but contract prices, vendor bills, square footage and supplies spend live in other systems with no shared location ID. Some losses are real; some are false losses from bad data.

How you would spot it in data Sites with no square footage on file, lumped vendor invoices that can't be matched to a location, supplies cost above the allowance in price, and no per-site margin report today.

9

Nobody owns site profit

Inside the facilities company

NO-OWNER

Every team is rewarded for something else, so nobody is in charge of whether each building makes money.

What goes wrong Sales is paid on revenue, account managers on happiness (so they give credits without charging the vendor), operations on coverage, procurement on filling every site, and inspectors visit the easy-to-reach sites.

How you would spot it in data No per-site margin in anyone's scorecard; credits with no vendor chargeback; inspections clustered near offices while far sites go unchecked.

Fix Name a margin owner for each zone and put site margin (M2) on the sales, ops, procurement and account-manager scorecards.

10

Wrong address or no access

When the crew arrives at the building

ACCESS-INFO

Sometimes the crew can't find the building or can't get in, and then the crew gets blamed for a missed clean.

What goes wrong Site details (the right entrance, block, floor, alarm code, key holder) are missing or wrong, so visits start late or fail. The miss is logged against the vendor even when the customer caused it.

How you would spot it in data Check-ins near but outside the site boundary, repeated 'no access' notes, and late arrivals clustered at the same sites.

Fix Your two-way on-time promise: a site passport at sign-up and a reason code on every late or missed visit.

11

Cleaners quit

Inside the vendor's crew

CREW-CHURN

When cleaners drive a lot without pay or get blamed unfairly, they quit, and new cleaners are slower and miss things.

What goes wrong Janitorial turnover is high. Unpaid drive time between scattered sites, low pay and blame for problems outside the crew's control push people out, and every new crew is slower and misses more tasks.

How you would spot it in data Roster changes and new names on check-ins, followed by a dip in inspection scores and a rise in tickets at those sites.

Fix Denser routes (less driving), fair reason codes, and inspection bonuses that reach the crew, while the vendor still manages its own people.

Q1

They asked: your problem framing and assumptions

Frame the problem

Every site is like a little lemonade stand. We need to know, for each one, if it makes or loses money, and why.

The profit equation

PER SITE, PER MONTH. All numbers are ILLUSTRATIVE.
M1 gross spread = customer revenue - vendor charges.
M2 contribution = M1 - credits - re-cleans - unbilled supplies - QA/field time. North star: report M2 dollars, M2 % and retained revenue together.
M3 fully loaded = M2 - activity-based account management, billing and platform cost - working-capital carry, where carry = (DSO - DPO) x cost of capital.
Vendor charges = visits x labor hours per visit x bill rate + travel and minimum charges + periodic work + premiums.
Should-cost = sq ft / production standard x bill rate x visits + travel allowance by density tier + periodic work.
Reference site: 10,000 sq ft office, 5 nights/week = ~21.7 visits. 10,000 / 3,000 sq ft/hr = ~3.3 labor hrs/visit = ~72 hrs/month x ~$29/hr (~$17 wage plus payroll taxes, insurance, supplies, vendor overhead and profit) = ~$2,100 vendor cost.
Healthy site: $2,600 revenue (~$0.26/sq ft) - $2,100 vendor = M1 ~$500 (~19%). Credits, re-cleans, supplies overage and QA time are near zero here, so M2 is also ~$500.
Losing twin (same building): legacy $2,300 price with no escalator, ISOLATED (50+ night drive-minutes). Vendor $2,100 + ~$450 travel and minimum charges = $2,550, so M1 = -$250 (-11%).
Losing twin M2: -$250 - ~$150 re-cleans and credits = ~-$400 (-17%); total cost ~$2,700 against $2,300 revenue.
Waterfall benchmark: the Core-tier should-cost (~$2,100) plus target margin, which is the $2,600 reference fee.
Bridge from healthy to twin: +$500 to -$400 = -$900 = -$300 price (UNDERPRICED: $2,600 - $2,300) - $450 travel above the Core allowance (ISOLATED) - $150 quality (RECLEAN-CREDITS), unexplained $0.
M3 adds working-capital carry: customers pay net 45-60 and vendors expect net 15-30, so we fund ~30 days of ~$2,100 vendor cost; at 10%/yr that is ~$17/month off M3.
Rules: a loss site has M2 below 0; a thin site has M2 between 0 and 15%. Any exit or rescope must raise account-level M2 dollars, and exits are never decided on fixed overhead (M3).

The profit tree

Each branch is a lever that can push a site into the red. Read top to bottom: margin splits into what comes in and what goes out.

  • Per-site margin ladder (M1, M2, M3)How much money is left from what one customer pays after we pay the cleaning helpers and fix their mistakes.M1 = revenue - vendor charges; M2 = M1 - credits, re-cleans, unbilled supplies, QA/field time; M3 = M2 - overhead and carry. North star is M2 dollars + M2 % + retained revenue together (illustrative: healthy ~$500 M2, losing twin ~-$400).
    • M1 gross spreadWhat the customer pays us minus what the helper company bills us.Revenue - vendor charges. Illustrative: healthy $2,600 - $2,100 = ~$500 (~19%); twin $2,300 - $2,550 = -$250.
      • Customer revenueAll the money the customer pays us for cleaning.Base fee + billed periodic work + approved extras. Illustrative reference site: $2,600/month (~$0.26/sq ft/month).
        • Contract price vs should-costIs our price at least what the cleaning should really cost?Compare price to should-cost (sq ft / production standard x bill rate x visits + travel allowance by tier + periodic). Illustrative: the twin's legacy $2,300 is $300 below the $2,600 should-cost-plus-margin price for this building, and $250 below its all-in ~$2,550 vendor cost before any margin. Code: UNDERPRICED.
        • Periodic work and extras billedExtra money for big jobs like waxing floors, or for chores the customer added.Strip and wax, carpet extraction, windows, approved change orders. A leak is any vendor-billed periodic or extra work with no matching customer charge. Codes: PERIODIC-GAP, SCOPE-CREEP.
      • Vendor chargesEverything the helper company bills us.Visits x labor hours per visit x bill rate + travel and minimums + periodic + premiums. Often one invoice total, so split it with should-cost and Peazy data (illustrative: healthy ~$2,100, twin ~$2,550).
        • Labor hours per visitHow many helper-hours each cleaning takes.Sq ft by space type / production rate (routine office ~2,500-4,000 sq ft/hr; restrooms and medical slower). Illustrative reference: 10,000 / 3,000 = ~3.3 hrs; observed proxy = geofence dwell x verified headcount. Codes: LOW-PRODUCTIVITY, SCOPE-CREEP.
        • Visits per month (billed vs verified)How many nights the helpers come, and whether they really came.5 nights/week = ~21.7 visits. Three-way match of scheduled, geofence-verified and invoiced visits, plus the short-visit rule (dwell x verified headcount < 60% of standard on 3+ visits in 30 days, illustrative). Codes: OVER-SERVICED, UNVERIFIED-VISITS.
        • Vendor bill rateHow much the helper company charges for one hour of cleaning.~$17 wage (about the BLS median for janitors) plus payroll taxes, insurance, supplies, vendor overhead and profit = ~$29/hr, about 1.7x wage (illustrative). A wide rate spread for the same work in one metro points to VENDOR-FRAGMENTED.
        • Travel and minimum-visit chargesPaying helpers to drive far, or paying a minimum fee even when the job is tiny.Travel share = drive / (drive + on-site minutes); red flag above 33%, target 15% or less in Core. Minimum visit charge $60-120 or a 1-2 hour floor; twin pays ~$450/month (all illustrative). Codes: ISOLATED, VENDOR-FRAGMENTED.
        • Periodic work costWhat we pay for big deep-clean jobs like floor waxing.Events x sq ft x periodic rate. Periodic recovery ratio = periodic revenue / periodic vendor cost; below 1 means PERIODIC-GAP.
        • Cover premiumsExtra money we pay when a backup helper must step in at the last minute.Backup-vendor step-in +15-30%; same-night emergency call-out 1.5-2x normal rate (illustrative). Track as % of vendor cost. Code: PREMIUM-COVER.
    • M2 deductions (cost of quality and leaks)Money we lose after the bill is paid, from fixing bad jobs and giving refunds.M2 = M1 - credits - re-cleans - unbilled supplies - QA/field time. Twin: -$250 - ~$150 = ~-$400 (illustrative).
      • Credits and re-cleansMoney we give back, or pay again, when a cleaning was not good enough.Failures per 100 visits x (re-clean cost + credit); charge back to the vendor when the miss is vendor-caused. Illustrative twin: ~$150/month. Code: RECLEAN-CREDITS (often fed by CREW-CHURN and ACCESS-INFO).
      • Unbilled suppliesExtra soap and paper towels the building uses beyond what the price includes.Supplies cost per restroom fixture or per occupant vs the allowance built into the price. The ~$29/hr rate covers supplies up to the ~$150/month allowance in the price; use above it is billed as overage (illustrative). Code: SUPPLIES-LEAK.
      • QA and field timeTime our checkers spend visiting this building and fixing problems.Inspector visits x (time + travel). Target inspections by risk, not by how easy the site is to reach (see NO-OWNER).
    • M3 deductions (fully loaded)Our own office costs to look after this building, plus the cost of waiting to get paid.M3 = M2 - activity-based account management, billing and platform - working-capital carry. Used for pricing and portfolio review; exits are decided on M2, never on fixed overhead.
      • Account management, billing and platformTime our office team spends on calls, bills and the app for this building.Allocate by activity (tickets, disputes, escalations), not evenly by site count, so noisy sites carry their true cost.
      • Working-capital carryWe pay the helpers before the customer pays us, so we are lending money while we wait.(DSO - DPO) x cost of capital. Customers pay net 45-60, vendors net 15-30; illustrative ~30-day gap on ~$2,100 at 10%/yr = ~$17/month.
    • Hidden forces (not a P&L line)Hidden reasons that make lots of costs go up at the same time.They show up through other lines, so tag them with their own codes and fix the cause, not just the symptom.
      • Customer-side access and site infoThe customer gives a wrong address or the door is locked, and the helpers get blamed for the miss.Reason-code every late or missed visit from geofence evidence; a complete site passport before service starts (Bhavani's idea). Code: ACCESS-INFO.
      • Vendor crew turnoverWhen helpers keep quitting, new ones don't know the building and miss things.Distinct crew members per site per month from the vendor roster; quality dips after crew changes. Code: CREW-CHURN.
      • No owner of per-site marginEveryone has a job, but nobody's job is to make each building earn money.Sales paid on revenue, AMs on satisfaction, ops on coverage, procurement on fill rate, QA inspects easy sites. Code: NO-OWNER.
      • Data trustSometimes a building only looks like it loses money because numbers were written on the wrong page.Missing sq ft, lumped vendor invoices, no shared location ID. Check before blaming anyone. Code: DATA-QUALITY.

Assumptions

  • "Losing money" means M2 contribution: the customer's fee minus vendor charges, credits, re-cleans, unbilled supplies and QA time, before head-office overhead.

    First we only compare money in with money paid out for each building.

  • Losses are concentrated: a minority of sites (illustrative: 10-20%) causes most of the loss dollars.

    A few bad buildings probably cause most of the trouble.

  • Most vendors are paid per site (per visit or flat monthly), with no reward for sites being close together.

    Helpers are paid building by building, so a far-away building costs extra.

  • Many sites were priced one at a time at signing, without a cost-to-serve model, and some legacy contracts have no price escalator.

    Some prices were guessed when the deal was made and never checked again.

  • Peazy records (geofence check-ins, completion, photos, inspections) can be tied to a location ID and are mostly reliable.

    We can trust the app's records enough to learn from them.

  • Contracts can be re-priced or re-scoped at renewal (often yearly), and customers value steady quality over the number of visits.

    Customers mostly want a clean building, not a certain number of visits.

  • The company can consolidate vendors within a region without losing customers, and vendors keep control of their own crews.

    We can change which helper company covers an area, but each helper company still runs its own team.

Q3

They asked: how you would identify root causes

Find the root causes

Like a doctor, don't just say the site is sick. Find out which part is sick: price, travel, too many visits, extra work, or a weak vendor.

The method, step by step

  1. 1. Build one trusted margin ladder per site

    Make one clean notebook page for each building that shows money in and money out every month.

    Technique Join customer invoices, line-level vendor invoices, contracts and Peazy (visits, geofence events, inspections, tickets, photos) on one shared location ID for 12-24 months. Accrue periodic work to its month, split lumped vendor invoices by should-cost hours, and allocate M3 overhead by activity, not by site count.

    Output Site-month table with M1, M2 and M3 in dollars and %, retained revenue, and a data-confidence flag per site.

  2. 2. Clean the data before blaming anyone

    Check the notebook for mistakes so we do not blame a building that is actually fine.

    Technique Run a three-way match (scheduled vs geofence-verified vs invoiced visits), check sq ft across contract, Peazy and public records, and apply the short-visit rule with headcount from the vendor roster plus a confidence flag. Tag false losses DATA-QUALITY and fix them first.

    Output Real-loss vs paper-loss list, quick recoveries from UNVERIFIED-VISITS, and a confidence score on every site.

  3. 3. Size the loss and put it on a map

    Line up all the buildings from most money made to most money lost, and put them on a map to see where the losers bunch up.

    Technique Draw a whale curve (cumulative M2 with sites sorted best to worst) and cut it by customer, metro, vendor, size, frequency and contract age. Assign density tiers by night drive-minutes to the nearest other site (Core 15 or less, Fringe 15-45, Isolated over 45, illustrative; MVP proxy = straight-line miles x 1.3 / ~30 mph) and roll losses up to the account.

    Output Loss-concentration chart, density-tier map, and a ranked loss list with M2 dollars at stake and a flag for sites inside profitable accounts.

  4. 4. Should-cost variance waterfall (primary attribution)

    Work out what cleaning each building SHOULD cost, then see which piece of the real bill is too big.

    Technique For each site compute should-cost = sq ft / production standard x bill rate x visits + travel allowance by density tier + periodic work, then walk actual cost to should-cost and actual price to should-cost plus target margin in dollar steps (price, frequency, scope, productivity, rate, travel, periodic, premiums, quality, supplies), each tagged with a root-cause code. Test UNDERPRICED against should-cost where sq ft confidence is high (peer price bands only where it is low), separate OVER-SERVICED from UNVERIFIED-VISITS with the dwell + scores + tickets + occupancy rule, and always report the unexplained loss.

    Output Per-site waterfall in dollars by code plus an unexplained bar. Illustrative benchmark: the Core-tier should-cost (~$2,100) plus target margin, which is the $2,600 reference fee; losing twin vs healthy site: -$900 = -$300 UNDERPRICED ($2,600 - $2,300), -$450 ISOLATED (travel above the Core allowance), -$150 RECLEAN-CREDITS, unexplained $0.

  5. 5. Prove the finder on a synthetic world (Bhavani's idea)

    Build a pretend world where we hide the problems ourselves, then check that our detective finds every one.

    Technique Simulate 2,000 synthetic sites, customers and vendors with known PLANTED problems across all 15 codes, run the exact same pipeline, and score recall and precision per code. Use the same digital twin to test levers before touching real sites.

    Output Recall and precision per code, the minimum data needed to catch each one, and a go/no-go before results drive real decisions.

  6. 6. Cross-check with ML and separate weak vendors from hard sites

    Ask a computer to look at all 2,000 buildings and see if it agrees with our detective, and compare helper companies fairly.

    Technique Train gradient boosting to predict M2 from site, contract, vendor, density and Peazy features and use SHAP only to cross-check the waterfall, with cross-validation and no cost lines that define margin as inputs; there are only a few hundred loss sites (about 300-400 if 15-20% of sites are negative, illustrative), so overfitting is a real risk. Fit a mixed-effects model with vendor and market effects so a slow vendor is not confused with a hard building.

    Output Agreement rate between waterfall and SHAP, a list of disagreeing sites for field checks, and a vendor league table with confidence intervals.

  7. 7. Group into archetypes and check in the field

    Sort the losing buildings into a few groups that are sick for the same reason, then go look at some to be sure.

    Technique Cluster loss sites on their waterfall shares (k-means or HDBSCAN), have ops managers name the clusters, and walk a sample of sites per archetype with vendor and customer interviews. Use geofence reason codes (vendor, traffic, customer info, no access) to confirm ACCESS-INFO before blaming crews.

    Output 6 validated archetypes with site counts, M2 dollars at stake and a matching fix playbook.

  8. 8. Pick pilots from the loss dollars, then test with clean controls

    Try the fixes in a few areas first and compare them with areas we left alone before changing everything.

    Technique Choose pilot zones and levers only after steps 1-7 show where loss dollars sit by code, with one hypothesis per archetype. Randomize at zone level (aim for 15-20+ zones per arm, illustrative) or use synthetic control, while Isolated sites are randomized at site level within each metro; keep control zones free of portfolio-wide changes until readout, and judge on M2 dollars, M2 % and retained revenue together.

    Output Pilot readout per archetype (M2 lift, quality, retention), plus monthly margin alerts and a should-cost guardrail on every new bid.

6 kinds of money-losing sites

Lonely or tiny site

ISOLATED VENDOR-FRAGMENTED PREMIUM-COVER

A building so far away or so tiny that the helpers spend more on getting there than on cleaning.

Symptoms Isolated tier (over 45 night drive-minutes, illustrative) or so small that cost per visit sits at the vendor minimum; travel share above 33%; trip, minimum and premium lines on invoices; misses with no backup nearby.

Likely cause Low density plus per-site minimums from a fragmented vendor base, with costly cover when the lone crew does not show.

Best fix Move Isolated and periodic work to the marketplace layer, offer route chains as suggestions the vendor's own dispatcher owns, switch to fewer longer visits, and add a travel surcharge or price floor at renewal. Exit only if account-level M2 dollars rise.

Stuck-price legacy contract

UNDERPRICED PERIODIC-GAP NO-OWNER

A customer still paying an old, too-low price while the helpers now cost more.

Symptoms Normal hours and good quality but price below should-cost; M2 % falling year after year; contract several years old with no escalator; periodic recovery ratio below 1.

Likely cause An old bid price never caught up with wages, and nobody owns per-site margin, so nobody pushed a reprice.

Best fix Reprice at renewal with a should-cost quote backed by Peazy proof of service, add a wage-indexed escalator, and price periodic work per event. Put a should-cost guardrail on every new bid and M2 into sales comp.

Creeping extras site

SCOPE-CREEP SUPPLIES-LEAK

A customer who keeps adding chores and using extra soap without paying for them.

Symptoms Dwell time rising with no scope change; vendor extra and supply lines with no matching customer charge; tickets and photos showing tasks outside the scope of work.

Likely cause Customer staff ask crews for extras and use more consumables, with no change-order or pass-through process.

Best fix Put the digital scope in Peazy so crews see only in-scope tasks, route every request through the app with an instant price and approval, and auto-bill approved extras. Move supplies to a pass-through or par-level model.

Over-cleaned quiet office

OVER-SERVICED

Helpers cleaning every night a room that hardly gets dirty.

Symptoms Short dwell + high inspection scores + few tickets + low occupancy; 'already clean' photos; for example 5 nights a week at a half-empty office.

Likely cause Frequency set by habit, not by how much the space is used.

Best fix Right-size frequency (for example 5 to 3 nights) and share part of the saving with the customer, so the fee drops but account M2 dollars rise (illustrative reference site: M1 ~$500 to ~$740).

Ghost-and-slow vendor

UNVERIFIED-VISITS LOW-PRODUCTIVITY VENDOR-FRAGMENTED

A helper company that is slow, skips visits, or charges for work it did not do.

Symptoms Invoiced visits above verified visits; dwell x headcount under 60% of standard (illustrative) on 3+ visits with falling scores or reused photos, or labor hours well above standard; the vendor-effect model points at the vendor, not the building.

Likely cause Weak vendor performance and no three-way match, made worse by many small vendors we cannot compare or lean on.

Best fix Pay for verified visits (auto-deduct only when there is no geofenced check-in at all), use vendor scorecards, and consolidate each Core and Fringe zone to an anchor vendor plus a named backup.

Re-clean magnet

RECLEAN-CREDITS CREW-CHURN ACCESS-INFO

A building where the job keeps failing, so helpers keep coming back to fix it.

Symptoms High failure and complaint rate per 100 visits, many return trips and credits, frequent crew changes, and late or missed visits with unclear causes.

Likely cause New crews who don't know the site, plus customer-side access gaps that get blamed on the crew.

Best fix Two-way on-time promise (Bhavani's idea): complete site passport before service, a reason code on every miss from geofence evidence, vendor-paid credits for vendor misses, and billable visits for customer-caused misses. Add photo checklists with no people in frame and free re-cleans in vendor contracts.

Q4

They asked: your solution and the role of technology

Fix it with technology

Instead of only asking helpers to charge less, change how the work is grouped, planned, priced and rewarded.

The levers

Density zones with an anchor and backup vendor

effort: high

Give all the buildings in one neighborhood to one helper team, with a second team ready, so they walk next door instead of driving across town.

How it works Tag every site by night drive-minutes to the nearest other company site (Core 15 or less, Fringe 15-45, Isolated over 45, illustrative), group Core and Fringe sites into zones, and re-bid each zone to one anchor vendor plus a named backup on a should-cost rate card, with the backup's step-in premium (+15-30%) agreed up front. The optimizer suggests aligned service days and night routes, the vendor's own dispatcher owns the final route, and the vendor keeps a share of the density savings.

Technology Geocoding and a night drive-time matrix (OSRM or a maps API, with the straight-line proxy in the MVP), capacitated clustering in Python, OR-Tools for route suggestions, and Peazy schedules plus geofence events to measure travel share.

AI's job Clustering and what-if runs (add or drop a site, see the zone's cost per visit) are tested first in the synthetic world (Bhavani's idea). People set the final zone boundaries because they know vendor footprints and contracts.

Fixes VENDOR-FRAGMENTED (travel and minimum charges at Core and Fringe sites), PREMIUM-COVER, LOW-PRODUCTIVITY, CREW-CHURN

Impact (illustrative) Targets vendor travel charges and minimum-visit charges ($60-120 or a 1-2 hour floor per visit) at Core and Fringe sites, plus rate gaps between vendors doing identical work. Illustrative: travel share falls from above 33% toward 15% or less in Core zones, removing 30-60% of those charges on re-bid sites, and premium savings are counted once, under Density zones; not additive with should-cost pricing or portfolio triage on the same travel dollars.

Risk Anchor dependence, capacity gaps and switching disruption; mitigate with the named backup, cutovers timed to renewals and a one-metro pilot first. Routes stay suggestions and tracking stays at site-boundary geofence events, which limits joint-employer risk.

Should-cost pricing and renewal repricing

effort: medium

Work out what each cleaning job really costs, including the drive and the big once-a-month jobs, and never charge less than that.

How it works Should-cost = sq ft / production standard x bill rate x visits + travel allowance by density tier + periodic work, so the reference site is 10,000 / 3,000 sq ft per hour x $29/hr x 21.7 visits, about $2,100/month (illustrative); a site is tagged UNDERPRICED against should-cost wherever sq ft confidence is high, and against peer bands only where it is not. 120 days before renewal the engine offers options: reprice with a wage escalator, add a remote-site fee, rescope, bundle with neighbors, or exit.

Technology A pricing service called from CRM/CPQ, contract metadata (renewal dates, escalator clauses), BLS and local minimum-wage feeds, and the site P&L in the warehouse.

AI's job A churn and price-sensitivity model picks the renewal option least likely to lose the account, and an LLM drafts the proposal backed by photos and inspection scores. National accounts are judged on account-level M2.

Fixes UNDERPRICED, PERIODIC-GAP, ISOLATED (remote-site fee)

Impact (illustrative) Targets the gap between today's price and should-cost plus target margin on legacy contracts without escalators, and unpriced periodic work. Illustrative: the losing twin, priced at $2,300, recovers $400-750/month ($300 to the $2,600 reference fee plus a $100-450 remote-site fee; +$400 is break-even M2), and renewing contracts gain 2-5 M2 points; not additive with portfolio triage on the same sites.

Risk Churn at renewal and Sales pushback; offer scope or frequency trade-offs, not only price rises. Report retained revenue next to M2 so repricing cannot win by shrinking the book.

Deal-desk margin gate

effort: low

Before we agree to clean a new building, a checker makes sure the price covers the cost and a helper team is close by.

How it works Every new quote or national-account site list gets an automatic should-cost, density tier and coverage check (an anchor or backup vendor within 45 night drive-minutes, illustrative) and scores Green (auto-approve), Amber (manager) or Red (VP plus a written strategic reason). Quotes cannot be submitted without sq ft, space mix, restroom count, floor types, periodic tasks and access windows, and sales commission moves from revenue to M2 dollars.

Technology CRM/CPQ approval workflow, the pricing service API, the zone coverage map, and a digital bid-walkthrough form.

AI's job An LLM pulls scope out of RFP documents and walkthrough notes, flags missing fields, and predicts likely scope creep by building type (for example medical offices adding sharps or biohazard requests).

Fixes UNDERPRICED, ISOLATED, NO-OWNER (sales paid on revenue), DATA-QUALITY (missing sq ft at signing)

Impact (illustrative) Targets loss dollars that new deals would add, not the existing book. Illustrative: near-zero new sites signed below 0% M2, and new deals at or above target M2, except deal-desk-approved exceptions.

Risk Slower sales cycles and reps working around the gate. Needs executive sponsorship and the comp-plan change in the same quarter.

Right-size frequency, periodic work and change orders

effort: medium

Clean busy rooms often and empty rooms less, and when someone asks for an extra chore, write it down and charge for it.

How it works Where the over-service test holds (short dwell plus high inspection scores, few tickets and low occupancy), offer a 'smart spec' such as 3 full cleans plus 2 touch nights instead of 5 full (illustrative), and move high dusting and floor care onto a priced periodic calendar; medical and food areas keep compliance frequencies. Any out-of-scope request or vendor extra needs an approved, priced change order before the vendor is paid for it.

Technology Scope and task library in Peazy, occupancy data where customers share it (badge counts, people counters), e-signature change orders, and a three-way match of scope, PO and vendor invoice in AP.

AI's job Models frequency against inspection scores and complaints to find quality-neutral cuts. An LLM sorts tickets and invoice lines into in-scope, billable extra or quality complaint and drafts the change order.

Fixes OVER-SERVICED, SCOPE-CREEP, PERIODIC-GAP

Impact (illustrative) Targets labor dollars above real need, unpaid extra tasks and unpriced periodic work. Illustrative: on the reference site, 3 full plus 2 touch nights cuts vendor cost by about $400-500/month (a touch night costs ~40-50% of a full visit: 3 full = ~$1,260 + ~8.7 touch visits at ~$39-48 = ~$1,600-1,680 vs ~$2,100, illustrative), with part shared with the customer so account-level M2 dollars still rise, and change orders recover 1-3% of revenue; not additive with should-cost pricing where a renewal option is a rescope.

Risk Short dwell can also mean skipped work, so never cut frequency on a site that fails the unverified-visits test, and occupancy data needs customer consent. Cost per visit rises when frequency drops, so judge it on cost per 1,000 sq ft per month.

Verified visits and outcome-based vendor contracts

effort: medium

Pay helpers for cleaning we can prove, give a bonus when the building passes inspection, and have them redo it for free when it doesn't.

How it works Pay on geofence check-in plus photos; a vendor-site goes to review when dwell minutes x verified headcount fall below 60% of standard labor minutes on 3+ visits in 30 days (illustrative; headcount from the vendor roster with a confidence flag), and pay is auto-deducted only when there is no geofenced check-in at all. Contracts add a small quality bonus pool, re-cleans at the vendor's cost, and an early-warning risk score per vendor-site so the named backup steps in at its agreed rate instead of a 1.5-2x same-night emergency call-out.

Technology Peazy geofence, photos and inspections, a computer-vision photo checker with perceptual hashing for reused photos, a vendor-risk score (rules first, gradient boosting later), a vendor scorecard portal, and AP rules that post bonuses and chargebacks.

AI's job Vision models score photo sets and catch missing, reused or off-site photos, and scores adjust for site difficulty so a busy clinic is not judged like a quiet office. In-person inspections go to high-risk sites.

Fixes UNVERIFIED-VISITS, RECLEAN-CREDITS, PREMIUM-COVER, LOW-PRODUCTIVITY

Impact (illustrative) Targets paid-but-unverified visits, re-clean and credit dollars (about $150/month on the losing twin) and premium cover spend. Illustrative: re-cleans and credits down 30-50% and premium spend down 30-50% (premium savings are counted once, under Density zones); not additive with the two-way on-time promise, which claims the miss-related share of credits.

Risk Vendor disputes and cash strain on small vendors; publish the scorecard rules and pilot before money changes. Tracking stays at site-boundary geofence events with consent, people stay out of photos, and per-worker check-in waits for a legal review of joint-employer risk.

Two-way on-time promise (Bhavani's idea)

effort: medium

The customer tells us exactly how to get in before we start, and when a clean is late we can show whose fault it was, so helpers don't get blamed for a locked door.

How it works Service cannot start until the site passport is complete (verified map pin, entrance photo, building/block/floor/suite, access instructions, contact), and the promise is 'cleaned and photo-verified before opening'. Every late or missed visit gets a reason code (vendor, traffic, customer info, no access) from geofence evidence; vendor-caused misses give the customer an automatic credit paid by the vendor, customer-caused misses are explained with evidence and stay billable under a clause agreed up front, and the site contact gets heads-up messages.

Technology Site-passport form in Peazy onboarding and the customer portal, geofence arrival events, a reason-code workflow, SMS or email heads-up messages, and contract clause templates.

AI's job A model suggests the reason code from geofence entry and exit events at the site boundary, site-contact messages and photos for a person to confirm. Image checks confirm the entrance photo matches the map pin.

Fixes ACCESS-INFO, CREW-CHURN, RECLEAN-CREDITS, UNVERIFIED-VISITS

Impact (illustrative) Targets credits and lost billing from late or missed visits, plus the premium cover and new-crew misses that crew churn causes. Illustrative: customer-caused misses become billable instead of credited, and the company's own net cost of misses falls 50-80%; not additive with verified visits on the same credit dollars.

Risk Customers may resist a billable no-access clause, so introduce it at new deals and renewals with evidence. Reason codes need a fair dispute path so vendors and crews trust them.

Supplies par levels and pass-through

effort: low

Keep a set number of soap and paper rolls at each building, like a fridge shopping list, and charge for anything extra the customer uses.

How it works Set a par level per site from restroom fixtures and occupancy and refill to par, instead of open-ended vendor orders. Usage above the allowance built into the price is billed to the customer as a contractual pass-through with a markup, and over-ordering without a usage reason is not paid.

Technology Supplies catalog and par table per site, smart dispenser data where available, vendor supply invoice lines matched to sites, and billing pass-through rules.

AI's job Forecasts consumption by fixture count, occupancy and season. Flags sites whose supplies cost per restroom fixture runs far above the allowance or peers.

Fixes SUPPLIES-LEAK

Impact (illustrative) Targets consumables spend above the allowance in price (unbilled supplies in M2). Illustrative: supplies cost back within about 10% of allowance, often $20-60/month per office site and more at restroom-heavy and medical sites.

Risk Customers may push back on pass-through charges, and running out of paper creates complaints. Keep a safety buffer and show usage data on the invoice.

Zone margin owner

effort: low

Give each neighborhood one grown-up whose job is to make sure its buildings make money, not just look clean.

How it works Name one internal owner per zone who answers for M2 dollars, M2 % and retained revenue together, and add M2 to the scorecards of Ops (today paid on coverage), Procurement (fill rate) and account managers (satisfaction). The owner works the negative-M2 queue, recovers vendor-caused credits, sends inspections to high-risk sites instead of easy ones, and watches M3 items such as working-capital carry ((DSO - DPO) x cost of capital), cutting DSO with proof-backed invoices rather than paying vendors later.

Technology Zone P&L dashboard on the M1-M2-M3 margin ladder, the negative-M2 alert queue, scorecard changes in the comp system, and a risk-ranked inspection scheduler.

AI's job Ranks the owner's sites by recoverable M2 dollars with root-cause codes attached. Drafts the weekly zone review.

Fixes NO-OWNER, RECLEAN-CREDITS (credits not recovered from vendors)

Impact (illustrative) Mostly an enabler whose savings are booked in the other levers; its own dollars are vendor-caused credits not yet recovered and the carry from customers paying net 45-60 while vendors expect net 15-30. Illustrative: recover 90%+ of vendor-caused credits and cut DSO by 5-10 days; not additive with verified visits or the two-way on-time promise on the same credits.

Risk An owner without decision rights over price, vendor and scope becomes a scapegoat, so give those rights plus a 10-business-day deadline on loss sites. Scorecard changes need HR and leadership backing.

Portfolio triage: fix, densify, rescope or exit

effort: medium

For a far-away building that keeps losing money, first check our math, then try a fair price, a closer helper or more neighbors, and only then hand it off politely.

How it works First rule out DATA-QUALITY false losses (missing sq ft, lumped vendor invoices, no shared location ID), then bucket each remaining loss site as Fixable (other levers), Densifiable (Sales fills the cluster), Strategic (keep, priced at account level) or Structural Isolated. Structural options are a remote-site fee, periodic-only service, a marketplace or circuit vendor, or a clean exit at renewal, and any exit or rescope must raise account-level M2 dollars.

Technology Site and account P&L, the zone map, a scenario simulator, the marketplace layer for Isolated work, and CRM prospect data for lookalike targeting.

AI's job A recommender scores each action by expected 12-month M2 dollars and churn risk, tested first in the synthetic world (Bhavani's idea). Lookalike models find prospects near orphan sites.

Fixes ISOLATED, UNDERPRICED, DATA-QUALITY

Impact (illustrative) Targets the M2 losses left after the other levers, mostly at Isolated sites. Illustrative: the losing twin moves from about -$400/month to break-even or better through a remote-site fee or a marketplace vendor, and fixing or exiting the bottom 5-10% of sites lifts portfolio M2 by 1-3 points; not additive with density zones or should-cost pricing.

Risk National customers want every site covered, and exits cut revenue. Weigh account-level M2 dollars and retained revenue before any exit.

Architecture, layer by layer

  1. 1. Data sources

    All the notebooks where we write down what helpers did, what customers paid us and what we paid helpers.

    Peazy (schedules, vendor activity, completion, geofence events, photos, inspections, service issues), billing/AR (invoices, credits, DSO), AP (vendor bills, extras, supplies, payment terms), CRM (contracts, price, escalators, renewal dates, scope), vendor master (rate cards, rosters, insurance, capacity), site inventory and site passport (sq ft by space type, fixtures, access details), and external data (BLS and local wages, drive times, occupancy where customers share it).

  2. 2. Integration and identity

    A sorting machine that gives every building one name and splits mixed-up bills so each building gets its own.

    Connectors (Fivetran or Airbyte) and Peazy webhooks; one golden Location, Vendor and Customer ID across systems; geocoding; OCR and LLM parsing of lumped vendor invoices and contracts with a parse-confidence score and human spot-checks; data tests (dbt tests or Great Expectations); and proxies with confidence flags (vendor-roster headcount, straight-line miles x 1.3 / 30 mph for drive time, illustrative) until per-worker check-in and a night drive-time matrix arrive.

  3. 3. Profitability warehouse and margin ladder

    One big scoreboard that shows, for every building, money in, money out and whether we win or lose.

    Cloud warehouse (Snowflake or BigQuery) with dbt facts for visits, invoice lines, vendor bill lines, inspections and tickets; a monthly site and account P&L on M1 (revenue - vendor charges), M2 (M1 - credits, re-cleans, unbilled supplies, QA/field time) and M3 (M2 - activity-based account management, billing, platform cost and working-capital carry); and a metric layer for should-cost, travel share, density tier and dwell versus standard.

  4. 4. Root-cause analytics and AI

    Detectives that study the scoreboard and write down which of 15 reasons makes each building lose money.

    Should-cost variance waterfall as the primary attribution into the 15 root-cause codes, with gradient boosting + SHAP only as a cross-check and unexplained loss always reported; rule tests such as the short-visit rule and the over-serviced versus unverified-visits split; LLM classification of tickets and invoice lines; computer-vision photo QA; vendor-risk and churn models.

  5. 5. Decision engines and synthetic world

    Advice machines, plus a pretend toy town with hidden problems where we check that the detectives find them before we try fixes on real buildings.

    Pricing service (should-cost + target margin + escalators), zone designer and route suggester (OR-Tools; routes are suggestions the vendor's dispatcher owns), frequency and scope optimizer, vendor allocation, triage recommender, and the synthetic world (Bhavani's idea): a simulated 2,000-site world with synthetic customers, vendors and planted problems that proves the root-cause finder recovers them and tests levers before real sites. Every engine proposes and a person approves.

  6. 6. Action apps, automations and guardrails

    Buttons and alarms that turn the advice into action, plus a referee that checks the changes really worked and that nobody gets spied on.

    Deal desk in CRM/CPQ, site-passport onboarding, renewal cockpit, zone margin owner dashboard, vendor portal (scorecard, zone bids, route suggestions, chargebacks), customer portal (proof of service, change-order e-sign), alerts in Slack/Teams or ticketing, and AP rules; plus pilot measurement against control zones, human overrides saved as training labels, and privacy rules (geofence events at the site boundary only, no continuous GPS, consent, no people in photos, legal review for joint-employer risk).

Problems that show up here

  • 1Contracts priced too low Some buildings were sold for less than it costs to clean them, so they lose money from day one.
  • 2Scope creep The customer keeps asking for extra jobs, the crew does them, and nobody pays for them.
  • 3Cleaning more often than needed Some buildings get cleaned every night even when hardly anyone is there.
  • 4Many small vendors, priced per site Lots of small helper companies each charge a minimum fee, so small or far-away buildings cost too much.
  • 5Too much driving Crews spend paid time driving between buildings that are far apart.
  • 7Re-cleans, credits and emergency cover When a job is done badly, someone pays to clean it again, and when helpers don't come, the replacement costs extra.
  • 9Nobody owns site profit Every team is rewarded for something else, so nobody is in charge of whether each building makes money.
  • 10Wrong address or no access Sometimes the crew can't find the building or can't get in, and then the crew gets blamed for a missed clean.
  • 11Cleaners quit When cleaners drive a lot without pay or get blamed unfairly, they quit, and new cleaners are slower and miss things.

They asked: how you would measure success

Keep score

A scoreboard tells us if the fixes work, and makes sure we don't save money by doing a worse job.

North star

Portfolio M2: dollars and %, with retained revenue

M2 contribution = customer revenue - vendor charges (routine, periodic, trip and minimum fees, premiums) - credits - re-cleans - unbilled supplies - QA/field time, summed over all ~2,000 locations for the trailing 90 days and refreshed monthly at billing close. Always report M2 dollars, M2 % of revenue and retained revenue (baseline revenue still active) side by side, so shrinking cannot game it: any exit or rescope must raise account-level M2 dollars, gains count only if all six guardrails hold, and M3 (fully loaded, including working-capital carry) is a check, not the target. Illustrative: the reference site's M2 is about $500/month (~19%), while its losing twin's M2 is about -$400/month.

We count how many dollars we keep after paying the cleaning helpers and fixing mistakes, and we check we kept our customers, so we can't look better just by getting smaller.

Guardrails: things that must not get worse

Customer churn per account: logo and revenue churn and renewal rate (core, in 2-page version)

Don't fix one losing building in a way that makes a big customer take all their buildings away.

Why Repricing or exiting one losing site can push a multi-site customer to move every building, so exits and price changes are judged on account-level M2 dollars, not one site. Rule (illustrative): churn no higher than control or the Phase 1 baseline, and zero accounts lost because of a pilot change.

Service quality: first-time inspection pass rate and complaints per 100 visits (core, in 2-page version)

Buildings still have to pass the white-glove check after we change how we clean them.

Why Fewer visits, denser routes and vendor changeovers can overload crews, and changeovers often cause a 30-60 day quality dip. Rule (illustrative): pass rate within 1 point of control, and complaints no higher at right-sized sites.

Vendor crew turnover and vendor health: crew turnover, vendor attrition and effective vendor rate per labor hour (core, in 2-page version)

If we squeeze the helper companies too hard or blame them unfairly, their helpers quit and the cleaning gets worse.

Why Squeezing vendors or blaming crews for customer-side misses drives CREW-CHURN, which brings slow new crews, no-shows and wage-and-hour risk. Rule (illustrative): crew turnover no higher than baseline, 90%+ of pilot vendors retained, and an effective rate above a floor that covers local wage (about $17/hr median) plus burden.

Customer satisfaction pulse (CSAT from site contacts: monthly pulse during the pilot, quarterly after)

The customer must still be happy, because saving money by cleaning badly doesn't count.

Why Margin earned by under-serving is borrowed against the next renewal. Rule (illustrative): no drop of more than 0.2 points on a 5-point scale vs control.

Urgent-issue response time (spills, floods, restroom outages, pre-event cleans)

When something spills, a helper still has to come quickly.

Why Fewer vendors per zone means less slack for emergencies, so the named backup vendor and the marketplace layer must keep response fast. Rule (illustrative): median urgent response within 4 hours and no worse than baseline.

Safety, security, privacy and joint-employer compliance incidents

Nobody gets hurt, nothing gets stolen, and we never spy on the helpers or take pictures of them.

Build and test first

Don't change all 2,000 sites at once. Build one simple tool, try the fixes in one city, and compare with a city you didn't touch.

Build first

Location Profitability Lens

A report card for every building that shows in green or red whether cleaning it makes or loses money, with a sticker that says why, like 'too cheap', 'too far' or 'cleaned twice'.

What it does Joins AR, AP, contracts and Peazy logs into a monthly M1/M2/M3 P&L per location, then explains each loss with a should-cost variance waterfall (sq ft / production standard x bill rate of ~$29/hr, built from a ~$17/hr wage plus taxes, insurance, supplies, overhead and profit, x visits + travel allowance by density tier + periodic work) that puts loss dollars into the 15 root-cause codes and always shows the unexplained remainder; gradient boosting + SHAP is only a cross-check. Tag rules (illustrative): UNDERPRICED = price below should-cost (peer bands only where sq ft confidence is low); a missing escalator is a risk flag, not a loss tag; PERIODIC-GAP = periodic recovery below 1.0; SCOPE-CREEP = unbilled new requests (LLM-sorted tickets); OVER-SERVICED vs UNVERIFIED-VISITS = the short-visit rule split by scores, tickets, occupancy and photos; LOW-PRODUCTIVITY = labor above ~125% of standard; ISOLATED = Isolated tier; VENDOR-FRAGMENTED = 3+ vendors in a zone; trip and minimum charges ($60-120 or a 1-2 hour floor) at Isolated-tier sites = ISOLATED, at Core and Fringe sites = VENDOR-FRAGMENTED; RECLEAN-CREDITS, PREMIUM-COVER and SUPPLIES-LEAK = those costs above set shares of revenue or the price allowance; ACCESS-INFO = customer-side miss reason codes or an incomplete site passport, or lockout, alarm and key tickets until reason codes exist; CREW-CHURN = roster turnover followed by misses; NO-OWNER = no named margin owner or no inspection in 90 days; DATA-QUALITY = missing sq ft, lumped invoices or no shared location ID.

Inputs MVP v1 musts: location-level AR and AP lines; contract price, frequency, scope, sq ft, escalator and renewal date; Peazy visits, geofence events, photos, inspections and tickets; geocodes; and production standards (~2,500-4,000 sq ft/hr routine office, slower for restrooms and medical). Proxies carry a confidence flag (crew size from the vendor roster, drive time = straight-line miles x 1.3 / ~30 mph), and Phase 2 musts are a night drive-time matrix and per-worker check-in (geofence events only, consent, legal review), plus site-passport fields as they are collected (Bhavani's idea).

Outputs (1) Monthly M1, M2 and M3 per location, with M3 including working-capital carry ((DSO - DPO) x cost of capital, as customers pay net 45-60 and vendors expect net 15-30), reconciled to finance within 2%; (2) a should-cost waterfall per site with loss dollars by code and the unexplained remainder, for example the losing twin vs the reference site: +$500 spread, -$300 UNDERPRICED, -$450 ISOLATED, -$150 RECLEAN-CREDITS, unexplained $0 = about -$400/month M2 (illustrative); (3) a ranked fix list where each code maps to one lever and one owner; (4) a density-tier map (Core, Fringe, Isolated) with vendor overlap per zone; (5) a vendor scorecard. (6) The Phase 1 baseline for the north star, 12 KPIs and 6 guardrails, plus a margin bridge to target with each dollar assigned to exactly one lever; (7) CSV exports for ops and sales.

Tech stack Python with pandas or Polars on DuckDB (spatial extension) or Postgres+PostGIS (2,000 sites x 24 months is under 50k P&L rows plus a few million visit events); an LLM (e.g. Claude) with JSON output parses lumped vendor invoices and contracts and sorts tickets into the fixed code list, with parse-confidence scores and ~5% human spot-checks; LightGBM + SHAP as cross-check only; Great Expectations data checks; Streamlit + pydeck for the map; a NumPy/Faker generator for the synthetic twin; OR-Tools arrives in Phase 2 for route suggestions the vendor's dispatcher owns. Idea B · a lever

Two-way on-time promise

Like Domino's promise, but fair both ways: if the helpers are late, the customer gets money back; if the customer gave a wrong address or locked the door, we show the proof.

  • Site passport at sign-up: verified map pin, entrance photo, building, block, floor, access steps and a contact. Service starts only when it is complete.
  • Every late or missed visit gets a reason code (vendor, traffic, customer info, no access) from geofence evidence.
  • Vendor-caused miss: automatic credit to the customer, paid by the vendor.
  • Customer-caused miss: shown with evidence, and the visit stays billable under a clause agreed in the contract up front.
  • Heads-up messages to the site contact, and add-on offers when a crew is already nearby (no extra travel cost).

Why it is strong It protects crews from unfair blame (pin 11), fixes missing site details (pin 10), shows customers the truth (pin 7) and puts a price on poor vendor work.

Watch out These customers are businesses cleaned at night, so the promise is 'cleaned and photo-verified before opening', not 'arrives in 30 minutes'. On its own it does not fix pricing, travel or vendor structure (pins 1, 4, 5).
